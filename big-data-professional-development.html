Professional Development

This section records evidence of my professional development throughout the Deciphering Big Data module. It includes team meeting notes, feedback received from peers and tutors, a professional skills matrix and a personal development plan.

The section particularly supports Learning Outcome 4 by demonstrating my development as a member of a virtual development team, while also providing evidence of communication, problem-solving, critical reflection and professional skills.

Team Meeting Notes

Team Meeting 1 – 9 August 2026

The first team meeting was held on 9 August 2026 to discuss the Database Analytics Design assignment brief and agree how the group would approach the project.

Attendees

  • Chika Nwankwo
  • Romario Barker
  • Maria Almiron
  • Emily Starling

Purpose of the Meeting

The purpose of the meeting was to review the assignment requirements, discuss the proposed project area and consider how responsibilities should be divided between the four group members.

Key Discussion Points

  • The group reviewed the requirement to design and propose a logical database supported by AI tools.
  • We discussed the need to identify appropriate entities, data items, relationships and associations for the database design.
  • The group considered how AI tools could support schema design, data cleansing, data analysis and the extraction of useful insights.
  • We discussed the requirement to propose an efficient data pipeline covering data capture, manipulation, retrieval and analysis.
  • The group also considered the need to critically evaluate the selected AI tools and their role within the data management pipeline.

Project Direction

The group agreed to focus on a healthcare-related database project using a hospital patient readmission dataset. This was considered appropriate because it provides opportunities to explore database design, data quality, data cleansing, analysis and the generation of useful healthcare insights.

Team Working

The meeting also considered how the work could be divided between the four group members. The aim was to ensure that each person had responsibility for a defined area while still contributing to the overall report and final design.

Reflection

This meeting was useful in establishing a shared understanding of the assignment and the expectations of the group project. Discussing the brief together helped identify the main areas of work and highlighted the importance of allocating responsibilities clearly so that the project could progress efficiently.

It also provided early evidence of working collaboratively in a virtual professional environment. Agreeing the project direction and discussing roles supported communication, teamwork and shared accountability, contributing directly to Learning Outcome 4.

Feedback from Peers and Tutors

Peer Feedback: Collaborative Discussion 1

Feedback on my initial collaborative discussion post was positive about my exploration of the benefits of IoT, particularly its application in healthcare, and my consideration of data quality and data cleansing.

The feedback also identified areas where my analysis could be developed further. I was encouraged to consider the limitations of traditional data-wrangling methods for complex IoT datasets, particularly where errors may accumulate over time, relate to other data or have several possible methods of correction.

I was also encouraged to examine the Mirai botnet in greater depth and consider the wider technical and regulatory challenges of securing consumer-managed IoT devices.

A further area of development concerned governance. The feedback encouraged me to consider the importance of government and industry collaboration in creating adaptable, resilient and secure IoT environments.

How I Responded to the Feedback

I incorporated these points into my final collaborative discussion summary. This developed my analysis beyond identifying IoT risks and helped me consider how data quality, security and governance challenges can be managed in practice.

This process reinforced the value of peer feedback as part of academic and professional development. It demonstrated that constructive feedback can identify gaps in an argument and support deeper critical analysis.
