Collaborative Discussion – Summary and Reflection

This collaborative discussion developed my understanding of how p-values, confidence intervals and effect estimates should be interpreted within the wider analytical context. Although my initial focus was on avoiding reliance on p-values alone, peer contributions highlighted that reporting additional statistical measures does not necessarily improve an analysis if the assumptions underlying the chosen method are inappropriate.

A particularly important learning point was that statistical software can produce technically correct calculations even when the selected method is not appropriate for the structure of the data. The discussion around repeated observations and the assumption of independence demonstrated how apparently precise statistical results can still be misleading.

The discussion also highlighted that confidence intervals can themselves be reduced to another significance test if attention is focused only on whether an interval includes a particular value. This reinforced the importance of considering the wider analytical context rather than relying on individual statistical measures.

Overall, my understanding progressed from focusing mainly on how statistical results are reported to considering the validity of the analytical process that produces them. Going forward, I will consider data quality and structure, statistical assumptions, potential confounding factors, effect size, uncertainty and practical significance before drawing conclusions from statistical outputs.
