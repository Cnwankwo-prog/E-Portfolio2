Unit 4: Probabilities, Probability Distributions and Sampling

Overview

This unit developed my understanding of probability and its importance within statistical analysis. Probability provides a way of describing uncertainty and can support decision-making when outcomes cannot be predicted with complete certainty.

The unit also explored discrete and continuous probability distributions, sampling methods and inferential statistics. These concepts demonstrate how information obtained from a sample can be used to make informed conclusions about a wider population when the sample is appropriately selected.

Learning Outcomes

Through this unit, I developed my ability to:

  • Apply the fundamental rules of probability.
  • Understand discrete probability distributions.
  • Understand continuous probability distributions.
  • Recognise when different probability distributions may be appropriate.
  • Understand the importance of sampling within statistical analysis.
  • Differentiate between simple random, stratified random, systematic and cluster sampling.
  • Interpret statistical findings while considering uncertainty and potential error.

Understanding Probability

The reading strengthened my understanding of probability as a measure of how likely an event is to occur. I also explored the addition and multiplication rules of probability and their application to mutually exclusive and independent events.

Understanding these rules helped me appreciate how probability can be used to quantify uncertainty and support the interpretation of real-world data.

Discrete Probability Distributions

A probability distribution describes the possible outcomes of a random variable together with the probability associated with each outcome.

I explored several discrete probability distributions, including:

  • Binomial distribution – useful when there is a fixed number of trials with two possible outcomes.
  • Poisson distribution – useful for examining how frequently an event occurs over a specified period or interval.
  • Hypergeometric distribution – applicable when sampling from a finite population without replacement.

Using the interactive Wolfram demonstrations allowed me to change different parameters and observe how these changes affected the resulting probabilities and distributions.

Continuous Probability Distributions

I also explored continuous probability distributions. Unlike discrete distributions, continuous distributions can represent values across a range rather than individual countable outcomes.

The distributions explored included:

  • Normal distribution
  • Continuous uniform distribution
  • Exponential distribution

The interactive activities helped me visualise how changing the parameters of each distribution affected its shape and spread. This made the distinction between discrete and continuous distributions much clearer.

Quantiles

The unit also developed my understanding of quantiles. Quantiles divide ordered data into defined proportions and can be used to describe the position of observations within a distribution.

For example, quartiles divide data into four sections, while percentiles divide data into 100 sections. These measures can provide useful information about the distribution and relative position of observations within a dataset.

The Central Limit Theorem

Another important concept was the Central Limit Theorem. Under suitable conditions, the distribution of sample means becomes increasingly similar to a normal distribution as the sample size increases, even when the original population is not normally distributed.

Understanding this principle helped me appreciate why sampling is so important within inferential statistics and why many statistical techniques can be applied to sample data.

Type I and Type II Errors

The unit also introduced the possibility of statistical error when conducting hypothesis testing.

  • Type I error: rejecting the null hypothesis when it is actually true.
  • Type II error: failing to reject the null hypothesis when it is actually false.

This reinforced the importance of interpreting statistical results carefully rather than assuming that a numerical result automatically provides a definitive conclusion.

Sampling

The unit explored how sampling enables conclusions to be drawn about a population without necessarily collecting data from every member of that population.

Sampling approaches considered included:

  • Simple random sampling
  • Stratified random sampling
  • Systematic sampling
  • Cluster sampling

This highlighted the importance of selecting an appropriate sampling method because the quality and representativeness of the sample can affect the reliability and generalisability of the conclusions drawn.

Reflection

Completing the reading alongside the interactive activities helped me understand probability in a more practical way. Rather than only learning definitions and formulas, I was able to change variables in the Wolfram demonstrations and observe how the probabilities and distributions changed. This was particularly useful for distinguishing between discrete and continuous distributions and understanding when different distributions might be appropriate.

One of my main learning points is that statistical results should not simply be accepted because a calculation produces a particular value. The type of data, choice of distribution, sample size and possibility of statistical error all need to be considered when interpreting the result (Bader and Leuzinger, 2025). This has made me more aware of the importance of critically assessing numerical evidence before using it to support a conclusion.

Overall, this unit has strengthened my understanding of the mathematical and statistical principles that underpin Data Science and AI. I now have a better appreciation of how probability can be used to describe uncertainty, analyse data and support decision-making. It has also reinforced the importance of interpreting statistical findings within their wider context rather than relying solely on numerical outputs.
